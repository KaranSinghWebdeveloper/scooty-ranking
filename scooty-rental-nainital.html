Visiting Nainital on a Rented Scooty

Nainital is India's most visited Kumaon hill station, famous for the crescent-shaped Naini Lake, the bustling Mall Road, and breathtaking views from Snow View Point and Tiffin Top. Located just 18 km from Kainchi Dham and 11 km from Bhowali, Nainital is one of the most popular day trips for visitors to the region.

However, Nainital's narrow roads and extreme car congestion (especially during summer and weekends) make four-wheelers impractical. A scooty is the smartest way to get there — you can bypass the long car queues on the approach road and find parking easily at Tallital or Mallital.

How the Rental Works for Nainital Trips

Our rental point is at Kainchi Dham (Main Market, opposite Neem Karoli Baba Ashram), 18 km from Nainital. Many visitors combine a morning visit to the Ashram with an afternoon trip to Nainital — the ride takes about 45 minutes through beautiful pine forests and winding mountain roads via Bhowali.

Pick up your scooty in the morning, visit the Ashram, then ride to Nainital for the afternoon. Return by evening. The entire day costs just ₹500–700 depending on the scooter model — far less than a taxi round trip which typically costs ₹1,500–2,000.

The Kainchi Dham to Nainital Route

The route follows the state highway via Bhowali:

  • Kainchi Dham → Bhowali (9 km, ~15 min): Smooth highway through a forested valley. Stop at Bhowali to fuel up at the petrol station — this is the closest pump to Kainchi Dham.
  • Bhowali → Nainital (11 km, ~30 min): A steep uphill climb with sharp hairpin bends. The road is well-maintained but narrow. During peak season, traffic moves slowly. On a scooty, you can safely pass stationary cars.

Road tip: The descent from Nainital back to Bhowali has several steep drops. Use engine braking (keep the throttle closed and let the engine slow you down) rather than constant brake pressure.

Places to Visit in Nainital on a Scooty

Naini Lake & Mall Road

The heart of Nainital. The 1.5 km promenade along the lake is a pedestrian-only zone, so park your scooty at Tallital (lake's south end) or Mallital (north end) and walk. Boat rides on the lake cost ₹200–400. The area is lined with restaurants, shops, and the historic Naina Devi Temple.

Snow View Point

Accessible by ropeway (₹300 round trip) from Mall Road, or by a short scooty ride via the motor road from Mallital. On clear days, you can see the snow-capped peaks of Nanda Devi, Trisul, and Nanda Kot. Best visited early morning for clearest views.

Tiffin Top (Dorothy's Seat)

A viewpoint at 2,292 meters with 360-degree views of the Kumaon hills and Nainital town below. Ride your scooty to the Barapatthar area, then trek 30 minutes uphill. One of the best sunset spots in the region.

Eco Cave Garden

A family-friendly attraction with interconnected caves named after animals (Tiger Cave, Bat Cave, etc.). Located on the road to Sukhatal, easily accessible by scooty. Entry ₹35 per adult.

Hanuman Garhi Temple

A Hanuman temple perched on a hilltop offering panoramic sunset views over Nainital. Accessible via a short ride from Tallital. The temple is especially popular during Tuesdays and Saturdays.

Scooty Parking in Nainital

Two-wheelers have a significant advantage in Nainital. While cars face severe parking restrictions (especially during peak season when entry is regulated), scooters can be parked at:

  • Tallital bus stand parking area (south end of the lake)
  • Mallital parking near the Flats (north end)
  • Near Sukhatal for Eco Cave Garden visits

Parking fees for two-wheelers are minimal (₹20–30) compared to cars (₹100–200).

Tips for Riding to Nainital

  • Start early — traffic builds up after 10 AM on the approach road. Leave Kainchi Dham by 8 AM for a smooth ride.
  • Carry warm layers — Nainital is at 1,938 meters and can be noticeably cooler than Kainchi Dham, especially in the evening.
  • Watch for one-way restrictions — Mall Road and some lanes around the lake have one-way traffic rules that change by time of day.
  • Check weekend restrictions — during peak season, Nainital sometimes limits the number of vehicles entering the town. Two-wheelers are usually exempt, but it is worth checking locally.
  • Return before dark — the Bhowali–Kainchi Dham stretch has limited street lighting and sharp curves. Riding after sunset is not recommended.
